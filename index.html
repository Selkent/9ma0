    The session loop

    Questions first. This is the whole method, and every chapter card runs it in this order.

    1. Dr Frost, cold

      Open the chapter's practice and attempt questions before you've watched anything. Getting it wrong here is the point: it tells you which of the subtopics below actually need the video.

    2. Stuck? That subtopic's Maths Genie video, only that one

      Not the chapter. Not the playlist. The one five-minute lesson for the thing that just failed. Then back to Dr Frost until it regenerates one you can do.

    3. Still stuck? ExamSolutions

      Same content, different voice. Sometimes that's all it takes. If it isn't, that subtopic is going in the mistakes log and you're moving on.

    4. Question Set, then PMT

      Maths Genie's chapter Question Set with answers is the exit test. PMT's topic-sorted past paper questions are what real marks look like. Do both before you call a chapter landed.

    5. Move on when the budget's spent, landed or not

      Every chapter has a burst budget. When it's gone, mark it Landed if it landed and Parked if it didn't. Parked chapters come back in pass two, in March, with the textbook. That's not failure, it's the plan working: you'll know by February what you don't know, instead of finding out in May.

    6. First sixty seconds of every paper: last page, count the questions

      Every past paper, every time, until it's automatic.

    Two triage scales

    Saad

    Rating against GCSE. Nothing means the topic doesn't exist for you. Some means GCSE covered a version of it. GCSE-solid means you could do the GCSE form today and it's the A-level extension that's new.

    Hammad

    Rating against what you had. Gone means nothing comes back on reading the title. Half there means you recognise it and could reconstruct it with a nudge. Still have it means you could do a question now.

    Same buttons, different meaning. The rating changes nothing about the order you work in. It sets your expectation for how many bursts a chapter should take, so a "Nothing" that lands in two is a win you can see and a "Still have it" that needs six is a flag.
